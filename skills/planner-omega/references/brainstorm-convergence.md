# Brainstorm capture and convergence

Use for messy notes, ongoing dictation, competing ideas, or a requested challenge. Preserve the user's intent before filtering it. Do not treat this reference as a required phase for an already clear execution task.

## 1. Capture before critique

If the user is still generating ideas, acknowledge briefly and retain continuity across messages. Preserve fragments, contradictions, emotional motives, and speculative ideas. Do not evaluate during capture unless asked. If the user asks to analyze, choose, or plan, capture is complete enough to proceed. Ask whether to continue capture only if the intent is genuinely unclear.

Extract an objective card: outcome, decision, beneficiary, reason for urgency, success measure, constraints, and non-goals. Mark inferred fields provisional. Distinguish the main objective from enabling goals, side projects, and conflicting goals.

## 2. Inventory and preserve the essence

Assign stable IDs such as `IDEA-1`, `CLAIM-1`, and `OPT-1`. Do not renumber surviving ideas each turn.

| ID | Faithful statement / useful original phrase | Type | Unique contribution | Links / uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IDEA-1 | [one meaningful proposition] | [value, option, action, question, constraint] | [what a generic summary would lose] | [objective, related claim, dependency] |

Separate factual and causal claims from assumptions, hypotheses, preferences, examples, and actions. Repetition is not independent evidence. Preserve motives, metaphors, qualifiers, and dissent where they change meaning. Remove filler without removing identity.

Group by meaning, not repeated words. Distinguish duplicates, variants, complements, contradictions, causal links, and an idea's implementation details. A merge must retain source IDs and unique qualifiers.

Give an early, compact essence summary: **core intent; distinctive insights; values; tensions; provisional thesis**. Invite correction only when a misread would change the decision; do not wait for routine approval.

## 3. Keep an auditable decision ledger

| Idea IDs | Disposition | Preserved insight / canonical idea | Reason | Test or reopen condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IDEA-1, IDEA-4 | MERGE | [shared idea plus unique qualifier] | [genuine duplication] | [changed scope] |
| IDEA-7 | TEST | [unusual mechanism] | [novel but uncertain] | [small experiment and decision threshold] |

Use `KEEP`, `MERGE`, `REFINE`, `TEST`, `PARK`, or `REJECT`. Reject for contradicted evidence, fixed constraints, irrelevance, or a clearly better alternative—not merely because an idea is unfamiliar or difficult. Park uncertain ideas with resurrection conditions. Do not silently drop rejected ideas' unique contribution.

## 4. Investigate claims that can change the choice

Research in decision-value order: claims that can reverse the recommendation; safety/legal/major-cost constraints; feasibility; differences between leading options. Do not research every sentence equally.

| ID | Claim | Evidence status | Inspected source / date | Limitation | Decision effect / next check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CLAIM-1 | [testable statement] | [supported, contested, unknown, etc.] | [source and exact support] | [sample/version/jurisdiction] | [option affected] |

Statuses: `verified` (direct, relevant support), `supported` (support with limits), `plausible` (indirect support), `contested`, `unsupported` (adequate search found no support), `false` (strong contrary evidence), `unknown` (insufficient information). Explain uncertainty briefly; status is not a guarantee.

Prefer relevant primary data, authoritative documentation, and appropriate reviews. Inspect sources rather than relying on snippets. For consequential claims, cross-check independently when possible. Separate observation, correlation, mechanism, prediction, and causal inference. Report disagreement and external-validity limits. Do not infer falsity from a failed search. If browsing is unavailable, keep source-dependent claims provisional and name the cheapest check.

## 5. Compare options without crushing novelty

Form a small set of genuinely different strategies. Include delay, no change, or a small test when viable. Each option needs a mechanism, beneficiary, resource need, dependency, risk, assumption, and first test. Combine options only when compatible.

Set criteria from the objective before comparing. Show **novelty/differentiation** separately from **feasibility**; also consider evidence, impact, cost, time, reversibility, and user values. Prefer explicit tradeoffs to decorative scores. If quantitative estimates exist, state their basis and uncertainty.

For the leading route, state the strongest case for, strongest case against, decisive unknown, ranking-change condition, and cheapest informative test. When useful, challenge with alternative causal explanations, base rates, a failure pre-mortem, stakeholder incentives, hidden dependencies, and harm to affected non-users. Let the challenge change the decision; do not append criticism and ignore it.

## 6. Converge and hand off

Return the recommendation, preserved essence, only material ledger entries, evidence gaps, counter-case, and next reversible action. Use the planner's existing task contract for dependencies, owners, gates, and state; do not start a second roadmap.

In follow-ups show only: current conclusion, meaningful change, decision needed if any, recommended default, and consequence. Ask fewer, better questions. Proceed on explicit assumptions for nonblocking gaps. Stop when the decision is actionable; do not require repeated confirmation or perfect certainty.

Record measurement and review triggers to continue, modify, pause, stop, or reopen parked options. If evidence is incomplete, deliver a provisional decision plus the first test—not “more research needed” without a next action.
