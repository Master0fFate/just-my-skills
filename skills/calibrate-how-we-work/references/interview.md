# Adaptive question bank

Select only an unanswered, useful question. Ask one at a time and follow the
answer. This is a menu, not a required form. Do not infer a diagnosis or hidden
motive from any response.

| Area | Useful question | What to make operational |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learning | What did you do the last time a difficult idea clicked? | Examples, diagrams, reading, building, practice; context and exceptions |
| Getting stuck | What happened the last time progress stopped, and what help worked? | Smaller step, new model, test, break, evidence, or a direct answer |
| Entry point | For an unfamiliar task, what helps you start? | Goal, model, example, details; familiar versus unfamiliar work |
| Decisions | When does more analysis stop helping you choose? | Reversible versus irreversible choices, uncertainty, decision threshold |
| Recommendations | When do you want one answer rather than alternatives? | Execution versus exploration, ranked options, supporting reasons |
| Explanation | Show an answer with the right amount of detail. What makes it work? | Answer order, density, jargon, examples, optional depth |
| Ambiguity | When should I infer and proceed, and when should I ask? | Low-risk assumptions versus consequential choices |
| Friction | What made you stop reading a recent answer? | Repetition, caveats, jargon, questions, weak recommendations, missing context |
| Criticism | What makes criticism useful? How should I respond when I am wrong? | Problem, impact, correction, tone; fact versus preference |
| Pressure | What should change when you tell me you are rushed or overloaded? | Fewer choices, one next action, checkpoints; no mood inference |
| Task structure | What helps you keep track of a large task? | Plan size, visible progress, unfinished branches, priorities |
| Misreadings | What do new collaborators often get wrong about your signals? | Only cues explicitly confirmed by the user |

## Short probes

- Vague answer: “What would I do differently in practice?”
- Abstract label: “Can you give a recent example?”
- Absolute rule: “When would that approach annoy you?”
- Conflict: “What determines which of those two modes you want?”
- Falsification: “What would be a wrong conclusion from these answers?”
- Overuse: “What do assistants overdo after learning that preference?”

Do not probe clear evidence just because a probe exists. Before drafting, show
only the strongest provisional patterns and ask which need correction.
